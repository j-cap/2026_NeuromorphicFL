\begin{abstract}
This report is a living technical knowledge base for a research direction that transfers the integrate--threshold--spike principle from neuromorphic control to distributed and federated optimization. The central idea is not to train a spiking neural network. Instead, ordinary stochastic gradients or more general local learning updates are treated as evidence signals that drive persistent leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) communication states. Communication occurs only when sufficient signed evidence has accumulated, and the global model evolves through sparse signed parameter-update events. The project is motivated by the hybrid-systems viewpoint developed for neuromorphic control, but must be distinguished carefully from established work on one-bit gradients, error feedback, gradient sparsification, lazy/event-triggered distributed learning, and event-based optimization. The experimental program progresses from scalar and vector quadratics through asynchronous heterogeneous optimization, descent-aware certificates, convex logistic regression, nonconvex multilayer networks, real-data Fashion-MNIST classification, and compact convolutional neural networks. The evidence shows that the temporal coordinate-event mechanism remains communication-efficient across this progression, while also exposing explicit limitations in geometry, certificate maintenance, asynchronous class-wise transients, hybrid numerical reproducibility, and late-stage event-update resolution. The consolidation phase then freezes the smallest supported method and resolves three algorithm-level questions: the complete operator is distinct but closely related to residual-pulse predecessors, it remains effective when driven by conventional multi-step FedAvg model deltas, and its communication advantage survives an explicit sparse bidirectional synchronization protocol. A final matched closest-baseline campaign further shows that the frozen Event-FedAvg method occupies a strong communication--performance operating point relative to tuned residual-pulse, error-feedback, sign, and dense alternatives. The remaining core task is therefore theoretical characterization of the frozen lossy stateful compressor before extraction of a separate manuscript.
\end{abstract}
