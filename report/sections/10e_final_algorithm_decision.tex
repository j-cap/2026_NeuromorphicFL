\section{Final Consolidation Decision Framework}
\label{sec:final-consolidation-decision}

The consolidation phase was introduced to answer whether the accumulated experimental program already supports a distinct and complete communication-efficient federated-learning method. The three core questions now have explicit answers.

\subsection{Completed consolidation sequence}

The executed sequence is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\text{freeze V1}
\rightarrow
Q1\ \text{novelty/equivalence}
\rightarrow
Q2\ \text{bidirectional protocol}
\rightarrow
Q3\ \text{FedAvg compatibility}.
}
\end{equation}
The next step is no longer another mechanism-discovery experiment. It is a final publication-level closest-baseline campaign using the now-frozen client-update semantics and bidirectional communication accounting.

The three answers support a positive, but deliberately qualified, overall conclusion:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\text{The present evidence supports a complete communication-efficient FL algorithm candidate.}
}
\end{equation}
The qualification concerns novelty breadth rather than empirical viability or protocol completeness. The method should not be presented as inventing accumulated gradients, threshold sparsification, sign communication, event-triggered learning, or residual memory. The candidate algorithmic contribution is the specific stateful lossy pulse communication layer identified by Q1, together with its demonstrated FedAvg compatibility and sparse bidirectional synchronization protocol.

\subsection{What Q3 changes in the final method definition}

Q3 removes the largest remaining ambiguity in the client-side input. The standard FL form now uses
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_i^r=w_{i,E}^r-w^r
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
    z_i^{r+1}
    =\rho z_i^r
    +\frac{p_i}{\eta}\Delta_i^r.
\end{equation}
The earlier gradient-driven asynchronous formulation remains a special case and an important analysis tool, but the final method is no longer restricted to FedSGD-like updates.

The strongest Q3 evidence appears at genuine multi-step local training. At $E=5$ and $E=10$, Event-FedAvg reaches the same predictive regime as dense FedAvg and the strongest tested compressed baselines while using substantially fewer uplink bits. The $E=1$ bridge is weaker, which prevents an unnecessary claim of uniform dominance for every local-work setting, but does not weaken the central FedAvg-compatibility conclusion.

\subsection{Remaining tasks are paper-closing tasks, not algorithm-discovery tasks}

The core consolidation questions are now resolved. The remaining work should be selected to make the final claim publication-ready rather than to redefine V1. The highest-priority tasks are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item run the final closest-baseline campaign with the frozen Event-FedAvg semantics, including the Strom-style Q1 predecessor, EF-TopK, sign/error-feedback compression, and dense FedAvg;
    \item attach Q2-style bidirectional accounting to those final baselines so that all communication claims use the same end-to-end protocol model;
    \item formulate the strongest theory that is appropriate for the frozen stateful lossy compressor, preferably a convergence or compression-error characterization that does not reintroduce the rejected hard-descent machinery;
    \item then create the separate manuscript LaTeX root and extract the concise method--analysis--experiment story from this living report.
\end{enumerate}

Further datasets, larger CNNs, adaptive resolution, clustering, personalization, filter/block events, and larger client fleets remain optional paper-strengthening directions. None is required to determine what the algorithm is.

\subsection{Transition criterion}

The research program should therefore transition from mechanism discovery to manuscript construction after the final baseline/theory closure. The report remains the single source of truth until that point.

The consolidated answer to the motivating question of Part~II is:
\begin{center}

The accumulated results support a complete communication-efficient federated-learning algorithm candidate based on persistent local evidence, sparse first-passage coordinate pulses, full reset, and an independently controlled model-update quantum. Its novelty claim must remain narrow because closely related residual-pulse compressors already exist, but its FedAvg compatibility and end-to-end communication advantage are strongly supported by the present experiments.
}}
\end{center}
