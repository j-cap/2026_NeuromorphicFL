\section{Algorithm Consolidation: Frozen Method Definition}
\label{sec:algorithm-consolidation}

The experimental program through Experiment~15A reached a point at which further open-ended mechanism changes would make it harder, rather than easier, to identify the scientific contribution. Part~II therefore freezes the smallest mechanism supported by the accumulated evidence and asks whether it is distinct, compatible with conventional federated local training, and complete under bidirectional communication accounting.

The consolidation phase is organized around three questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Distinctiveness:} Is the final stateful event encoder mathematically distinct from the closest existing sign, error-feedback, sparsification, and event-triggered methods?
    \item \textbf{Protocol completeness:} Does the communication advantage survive when both uplink and downlink communication are accounted for in an explicit server--client synchronization protocol?
    \item \textbf{FL compatibility:} Does the same event encoder remain effective when its input is a conventional multi-step local-training update as in FedAvg, rather than one stochastic-gradient completion at a time?
\end{enumerate}
Q1 gives a qualified pass, Q2 a strong pass, and Q3 a strong pass. The method definition below therefore incorporates the standard FedAvg instantiation established by Q3 while retaining the earlier asynchronous gradient-driven formulation as a special case.

\subsection{Frozen Algorithm V1}

Let $u_i^r\in\R^d$ denote a local learning update produced by client $i$ at logical update index or round $r$. Each client maintains a persistent communication state $z_i^r\in\R^d$ and updates it according to
\begin{equation}
    z_i^{r+1}=\rho z_i^r+\alpha_i^r u_i^r,
    \label{eq:consolidated-state}
\end{equation}
where $\rho\in[0,1]$ is the evidence-memory factor and $\alpha_i^r$ converts the chosen client update into the evidence scale used by the encoder.

For conventional synchronous FedAvg, Q3 establishes the standard client update
\begin{equation}
    u_i^r=\Delta_i^r=w_{i,E}^r-w^r
\end{equation}
and the scale
\begin{equation}
    \boxed{
    \alpha_i^r=\frac{p_i}{\eta},
    }
    \label{eq:consolidated-fedavg-scale}
\end{equation}
where $p_i$ is the FedAvg aggregation weight and $\eta$ is the local SGD step. Since
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_i^r\approx-\eta\sum_{e=0}^{E-1}g_{i,e}^r,
\end{equation}
this choice yields
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_i^r\Delta_i^r
    \approx
    -p_i\sum_{e=0}^{E-1}g_{i,e}^r.
\end{equation}
The factor $1/\eta$ is therefore a deterministic representation conversion rather than an $E$-specific tuning parameter. In the asynchronous FedSGD-like experiments of Part~I, $u_i^r$ is instead a stochastic gradient contribution and $\alpha_i^r$ contains the client weight and compute-rate normalization.

A coordinate event is generated when
\begin{equation}
    |z_{ij}^{r+1}|\geq\Delta_j.
\end{equation}
The transmitted event is
\begin{equation}
    e=(j,s),
    \qquad
    s=\sign(z_{ij}^{r+1}),
\end{equation}
which contains a coordinate address and one sign bit. The corresponding communication-state coordinate is reset after transmission,
\begin{equation}
    z_{ij}^{r+1}\leftarrow0,
\end{equation}
and the server applies the sparse signed model jump
\begin{equation}
    w_j^+=w_j+q_r s.
    \label{eq:consolidated-server-jump}
\end{equation}
The event resolution $q_r>0$ is independent of the trigger threshold and the evidence-memory dynamics. Experiment~15A shows that this separation is important: persistent evidence memory can remain useful while the applied model quantum becomes progressively finer during late-stage optimization.

The frozen V1 mechanism is therefore
\begin{center}
\resizebox{0.96\linewidth}{!}{$\displaystyle \boxed{
\text{persistent temporal evidence}
+\text{coordinate first-passage events}
+\text{address/sign payload}
+\text{full reset}
+\text{independently annealed update resolution}.
}$}
\end{center}
Its standard FL instantiation is Event-FedAvg, in which multi-step local model deltas drive this stateful pulse encoder.

\subsection{What is deliberately excluded from V1}

The consolidation algorithm does \emph{not} include several mechanisms investigated earlier in the project. Homeostatic thresholds, strict coordinate competition, timing-derived jump rules, hard local or global descent certificates, layer-specific threshold normalization, filter-level events, clustering, and personalization are excluded because the experiments did not establish that they are required for the final empirical mechanism. V1 is therefore not the union of all ideas tested in Part~I, but the smallest mechanism that survived the sequence of falsification and scaling experiments.

Adaptive event-resolution control is also not part of V1. Experiment~15A demonstrates that stronger prescribed annealing is necessary at longer horizons, but the adaptive replacement proposed as Experiment~15B has not been established. The frozen method therefore retains a prescribed $q_r$ schedule.

\subsection{Communication interpretation}

The event encoder is best viewed as a \emph{stateful temporal compressor} rather than as a stand-alone optimizer. The client-side state integrates learning evidence until a coordinate reaches first passage. Unlike a memoryless sign compressor, communication is not mandatory at every local update. Unlike fixed-budget Top-$K$ sparsification, the number and identity of transmitted coordinates emerge from the event process. Unlike residual-conserving compressors, fired evidence is fully reset and the applied model quantum is not required to reconstruct the accumulator magnitude.

Q1 shows that the broad accumulate--threshold--pulse idea is prior art and that the defensible distinction is narrower: V1 is a leaky, lossy full-reset pulse encoder whose model quantum is decoupled from its communication threshold. Q2 shows that the same sparse server-update stream supports exact replay plus checkpoint fallback. Q3 shows that the encoder accepts conventional multi-step FedAvg deltas without pathological event growth. These three results define the object that should be carried into the final baseline campaign and manuscript.
