\begin{abstract}
\coreRev{Event-FedAvg reduces federated communication by converting persistent,
leaky client evidence into signed coordinate events. Fired coordinates reset
fully; an independently scheduled model quantum scales the server aggregate.
Lossless support encoding and aggregate-first downlink encoding synchronize
clients exactly within conventional synchronous rounds. We prove bounded
encoder state and a pathwise event budget, and derive a finite-horizon
gradient-norm bound conditional on expected aggregate alignment. Two CIFAR-10
architectures are evaluated for 1,800 rounds over ten paired seeds against
dataset- and architecture-calibrated Dense FedAvg, STC, Str\"om-style residual
pulses, and EF-TopK. Event-FedAvg has the lowest final bidirectional unicast
cost among the five selected operating points on both architectures.
At the declared 50\% accuracy target, it saves 57.8\% of traffic versus STC
on the compact CNN and 23.4\% on ResNet-14; savings versus EF-TopK are 95.8\%
and 65.4\%, respectively. Event also has the highest mean final compact-CNN
accuracy. On ResNet-14, it reaches $75.47\pm0.57\%$ at 5.752~Gbit, while
STC reaches $76.19\pm1.18\%$ at 6.460~Gbit. These results establish a favorable
communication-to-accuracy tradeoff at the calibrated operating points, with
explicit final-quality tradeoffs, rather than unconditional convergence or
global dominance of a method's full rate--quality frontier.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Federated learning (FL) allows a fleet of clients to improve a shared model
without collecting their raw data at one location. Keeping data local,
however, does not make the optimization process communication-free. Every
training round may require high-dimensional client uploads followed by a server
message that brings all clients to the next common model. For bandwidth-limited
edge systems, this repeated exchange can become the main scaling bottleneck of
federated averaging (FedAvg) \cite{mcmahan2017fedavg}.

\mOneRev{Cross-round accumulation is already used in error feedback and
coordinate residual thresholding
\cite{stich2018memory,richtarik2021ef21,strom2015distributed}.
These methods retain untransmitted corrections for later application.
Here we study an encoder that intentionally discards part of its accumulated
evidence through leakage and full reset. The question is whether this choice,
combined with signed coordinate messages and separately scheduled server steps,
can provide a favorable bidirectional communication--accuracy tradeoff while
maintaining exact synchronization. Communication savings and optimization
behavior must therefore be assessed together.}

\spacerev{Event-FedAvg is a stateful communication layer for a conventional
model and optimizer. Inspired by leaky integrate-and-fire neurons
\cite{gerstner2014neuronal}, its client encoder retains part of a parameter-wise
state, adds new local evidence, emits an index and sign at a threshold, and
resets. An independent \emph{model quantum} determines how far each net event
moves the server parameter. Only coordinate-payload generation is event-driven.
The full-participation protocol remains synchronous; sparse client
sampling is separately evaluated.}

\coreRev{With full participation, lossless encoding of client pulse positions
reduces upload cost. The server first aggregates coincident pulses, then
encodes the integer counts and any floating-point fidelity corrections.
This returns the exact update to every client without retransmitting each
client's pulse list. A smaller exact ordered representation remains available.
Partial participation is studied separately with explicit catch-up costs.}

\coreRev{We provide three contributions: (i) a complete synchronous signed-event
protocol with leaky evidence, full reset, independent model quanta, and exact
bidirectional transport; (ii) pathwise state and event-budget bounds, an exact
alignment decomposition, and a conditional finite-horizon gradient-norm bound;
and (iii) two calibrated five-method, ten-seed CIFAR-10 panels with conservative
unicast accounting. Event-FedAvg uses the least total communication at the
selected final operating points and has lower paired geometric-mean cost to
all three declared accuracy targets than every comparator. The evaluation
separates this communication advantage from final predictive quality, and a
controlled mechanism audit supports persistence at one operating point.
Figure~\ref{fig:event-fedavg-method} summarizes the protocol.}

\section{\rev{Related Work}}
\label{sec:related-work}



\mOneRev{One-bit, sign, and sparse methods reduce message precision or density,
while error feedback retains untransmitted corrections
\cite{seide2014onebit,bernstein2018signsgd,stich2018memory,richtarik2021ef21}.
Str\"om's residual-pulse method is the closest coordinate-encoder predecessor:
it accumulates gradients, emits signed threshold-sized updates, and subtracts
the transmitted quantum so that overshoot remains
\cite{strom2015distributed}. Event-FedAvg leaks gradient-scale evidence and
sets a fired coordinate's entire state to zero. Its server applies a
separately scheduled model quantum. These choices specify a different operator;
their individual predictive advantages are not established by the protocol
comparison.}

\mOneRev{Full clearing of accumulated memory also appears in LENA: its basic
algorithm clears an error vector on upload, transmits a vector-valued
correction, and uses drift information for silent workers
\cite{ghadikolaei2021lena}. Sparse ternary compression (STC) already combines
client/server residuals, sparse indices and signs, a data-dependent magnitude,
and bidirectional FL communication~\cite{sattler2020stc}.
SPARQ-SGD triggers compressed model-difference messages from vector deviations
and updates model estimates used for neighbor consensus
\cite{singh2023sparq}. Other event-triggered methods likewise regulate model
or gradient messages~\cite{he2023etfl,yoon2025etfl,zhai2026gossip}.
Table~\ref{tab:structural-comparison} separates the transmission rule, state
update, message representation, and coordination in the original protocols.
Accumulation, thresholding, full clearing, and sparse downlink are established
ingredients. We assess their specified combination here. \coreRev{STC, residual pulses,
EF-TopK, and Dense FedAvg form the numerical core; LENA and SPARQ-SGD supply
structural context because their original communication/coordination rules
differ from this numerical host.}}

conventional model.}

\section{Event-FedAvg}
\label{sec:method}

\spacerev{We first define the full-participation communication
operator. The partial-participation extension is specified below.}

\subsection{Round notation and local update}

Let rounds be indexed by $r\in\{1,\ldots,R\}$, clients by
$i\in\{1,\ldots,M\}$, and model coordinates by $j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}$, where a
coordinate is one scalar model parameter.
The server model at the start of round $r$ is
$\vect{w}^r\in\mathbb{R}^d$, and every client starts from this same model.
Client $i$ has local objective $F_i$ and aggregation weight $p_i>0$, with
$\sum_i p_i=1$. \newrev{The weights are protocol parameters known to the
server and clients before training. All reported client partitions are equally weighted, so $p_i=1/M$;
the partial-participation extension corrects selected contributions for
sampling.}
The global objective is
\begin{equation}
 F(\vect{w})=\sum_{i=1}^M p_iF_i(\vect{w}).
\end{equation}
Let $\vect{w}_{i,e}^r$ denote client $i$'s local model after local step $e$ in
round $r$. After setting $\vect{w}_{i,0}^r=\vect{w}^r$, the client performs
$E_r$ stochastic-gradient (SGD) steps with learning rate $\eta_r>0$:
\begin{align}
 \vect{w}_{i,e+1}^r&=\vect{w}_{i,e}^r-\eta_r\vect{g}_{i,e}^r,
 \qquad e=0,\ldots,E_r-1, \notag\\
 \vect{\delta}_i^r&=\vect{w}_{i,E_r}^r-\vect{w}^r
 =-\eta_r\sum_{e=0}^{E_r-1}\vect{g}_{i,e}^r .
 \label{eq:local-delta}
\end{align}
Here $\vect{g}_{i,e}^r$ is the stochastic gradient at local model
$\vect{w}_{i,e}^r$. The vector $\vect{\delta}_i^r$ is the complete local model
change supplied to the communication layer.

\subsection{Client event encoder}

\spacerev{Client $i$ carries one post-reset encoder state
$\vect{z}_i^r\in\mathbb{R}^d$ across rounds, initialized as
$\vect{z}_i^1=\vect{0}$. This state accumulates weighted, gradient-scale local
changes before thresholding. The client retains $\vect{z}_i^{r+1}$, while every
client receives the synchronized model $\vect{w}^{r+1}$.}

The weighted evidence input $\vect{a}_i^r$ and state
immediately before the threshold test, $\widetilde{\vect{z}}_i^r$, are
\begin{equation}
 \vect{a}_i^r=\frac{p_i}{\eta_r}\vect{\delta}_i^r,
 \qquad
 \widetilde{\vect{z}}_i^r=\rho_r\vect{z}_i^r+\vect{a}_i^r,
 \quad 0\leq\rho_r\leq1 .
 \label{eq:evidence-update}
\end{equation}
\spacerev{The weight $p_i$ is applied before thresholding and not again at the
server. Dividing by $\eta_r$ gives the gradient-scale input
$\vect{a}_i^r=-p_i\sum_e\vect{g}_{i,e}^r$. The leak $\rho_r$ retains part of the
preceding state. For threshold $\vartheta>0$, each parameter is tested once at
round end:}
\vspace{-2pt}
\begin{align}
 m_{ij}^r&=\ind{|\widetilde z_{ij}^r|\geq\vartheta},
 &c_{ij}^r&=m_{ij}^r\operatorname{sign}(\widetilde z_{ij}^r),
 \label{eq:event-pulse}\\
 \vect{z}_i^{r+1}&=(\vect{1}-\vect{m}_i^r)\odot
 \widetilde{\vect{z}}_i^r,
 &\vect{c}_i^r&\in\{-1,0,1\}^d .
 \label{eq:full-reset}
\end{align}
\vspace{-2pt}
\spacerev{The mask $\vect{m}_i^r$ records fired parameters, and
$\vect{c}_i^r$ contains their signed pulses. A fire is the first sampled
crossing after a reset. Equation~\eqref{eq:full-reset} discards the full
pre-trigger state, including overshoot, rather than subtracting the threshold
or preserving a compression residual
\cite{stich2018memory,strom2015distributed}.}

\vspace{-3pt}
\subsection{Server update and synchronization}

The server sums the client pulses into $\vect{C}^r\in\mathbb{Z}^d$. The positive
model quantum $q_r$ is the server-model change caused by one net signed event.
It is common to all \newrev{parameters} in a round but independent of $\vartheta$:
\vspace{-2pt}
\begin{equation}
 \vect{C}^r=\sum_{i=1}^M\vect{c}_i^r,
 \qquad
 \vect{w}^{r+1}=\vect{w}^r+q_r\vect{C}^r .
 \label{eq:server-update}
\end{equation}
\vspace{-2pt}
\spacerev{We use $q_r=q_0(1+r/r_0)^{-\alpha}$, where $q_0>0$, $r_0>0$, and
$\alpha\geq0$. Clients infer $q_r$ from the round index, so an event carries no
floating-point magnitude. Pulses for the same parameter and round reinforce
when their signs agree and cancel otherwise.}

\coreRev{Client pulses use an exact integer frame with a 128-bit header.
The encoder selects the shortest byte-padded representation from Rice-coded
position gaps, explicit indices, bitmap support, and dense alternatives.
For signed unit pulses, the selected payload carries support and signs, with
no floating-point magnitude per coordinate. Empty client supports are silent.}

\coreRev{The downlink encodes the aggregate integer count vector rather than
concatenating client messages. In real arithmetic, this vector and the known
$q_r$ specify~\eqref{eq:server-update}. The implementation preserves its native
ordered float32 update: when canonical count scaling differs in the last bits,
a sparse mask and float32 fidelity corrections are included and charged.
The server chooses the smaller exact counts-plus-corrections frame or an exact
ordered-pulse frame. Decoding reproduces the update bit for bit; this transport
step adds no second lossy optimization operator.}

\coreRev{Primary accounting sums all serialized upload bytes, metadata and
padding, initial model synchronization, 32-bit per-client round requests, and
a separate copy of each server message for each recipient. Logical broadcast,
which charges a shared server payload once, is supplementary. These modeled
payload costs do not include a physical network's transport headers or latency.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:event-fedavg} states the ordering.}

\begin{samepage}
\noindent\rule{\columnwidth}{0.8pt}
\begin{algorithm}[Event-FedAvg communication layer]
\label{alg:event-fedavg}
\normalfont\small
Initialize $\vect{w}^1$ and $\vect{z}_i^1=\vect{0}$. The server supplies
$p_i$, $\vartheta$, and the schedules $\eta_r$, $\rho_r$, and $q_r$. For each
round $r=1,\ldots,R$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Local learning:} Every client starts from $\vect{w}^r$,
  performs $E_r$ local SGD steps, and forms $\vect{\delta}_i^r$
  using~\eqref{eq:local-delta}.
  \item Client $i$ computes
  $\widetilde{\vect{z}}_i^r=\rho_r\vect{z}_i^r+
  (p_i/\eta_r)\vect{\delta}_i^r$ and forms $\vect{m}_i^r$ and
  $\vect{c}_i^r$ using~\eqref{eq:event-pulse}.
  \item Client $i$ resets the fired states using~\eqref{eq:full-reset} and
  \coreRev{sends an exactly encoded support and signs of the nonzero entries
  of $\vect{c}_i^r$.}
  \item \textbf{Server update:} The server forms
  $\vect{C}^r=\sum_i\vect{c}_i^r$ and sets
  $\vect{w}^{r+1}=\vect{w}^r+q_r\vect{C}^r$.
  \item \textbf{Synchronization after every round:} \coreRev{The server returns the smaller exact aggregate-with-corrections
  or ordered-pulse representation, including for an empty aggregate.
  Every client then holds $\vect{w}^{r+1}$.}
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}
\vspace{-2pt}
\noindent\rule{\columnwidth}{0.8pt}
\end{samepage}

\noindent\textbf{Partial-participation extension.}
Let $\mathcal P_r$ contain the selected clients, with inclusion
probabilities $\pi_i=\Pr(i\in\mathcal P_r)$. Only selected clients
train and emit; their pre-threshold evidence uses
$(p_i/\pi_i)\vect{\delta}_i^r/\eta_r$.
For fixed potential local changes at a common server model,
the unthresholded sampled sum is unbiased:
$\E_{\mathcal P_r}[\sum_{i\in\mathcal P_r}
(p_i/\pi_i)\vect{\delta}_i^r]=\sum_i p_i\vect{\delta}_i^r$.
This identity does \emph{not} extend to the emitted event aggregate:
the stateful threshold, sign, and full reset are nonlinear,
and inactive clients emit nothing. The server sums only selected
events without applying a second sampling weight.
Inactive encoder states persist; upon return after $g$ rounds,
a client applies $\rho^g$ before adding new evidence.
Selected clients synchronize exactly before training and after
aggregation; terminal catch-up of stale clients is charged to traffic.
The theory below concerns full participation
($\mathcal P_r=\{1,\ldots,M\}$), not convergence under sampling.

\section{\rev{Finite-Horizon Communication and Alignment Analysis}}
\label{sec:theory}

\newrev{We analyze the event budget, realized gradient alignment,
and a conditional finite-horizon optimization bound.}

\newrev{For the first $R$ rounds, let $N_R^{\rm coord}$ denote the total number of
client-coordinate events. Every fired coordinate of every client contributes
one count, even when another client's opposite event later cancels it at the
server:}
\begin{equation}
 \newrev{N_R^{\rm coord}=\sum_{r=1}^R\sum_{i=1}^M\sum_{j=1}^d m_{ij}^r.}
 \label{eq:coordinate-event-count}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[State and pathwise event budget]
Assume $|z_{ij}^1|<\vartheta$ initially. The full-reset encoder keeps
$|z_{ij}^r|<\vartheta$ in every round. \rev{The total in
\eqref{eq:coordinate-event-count} satisfies}
\begin{equation}
 N_R^{\rm coord}\leq\frac{1}{\vartheta}
 \left(\sum_i\norm{\vect{z}_i^1}_1+
 \sum_{r=1}^R\sum_i\norm{\vect{a}_i^r}_1\right).
 \label{eq:event-budget}
\end{equation}
\coreRev{Let $P_R$ count nonempty client packets and
$b_d=\lceil\log_2d\rceil$. The serialized event uplink satisfies}
\begin{equation}
 \coreRev{U_R\leq135P_R+(b_d+1)N_R^{\rm coord}.}
 \label{eq:encoded-uplink-bound}
\end{equation}
\coreRev{The bound includes 128 header bits and at most seven padding bits
per packet: the codec can always select explicit indices plus signs.
It concerns client event payloads; synchronization and request costs are
accounted separately.}
\end{proposition}
The threshold test and reset prove the state claim. Partition each \newrev{coordinate-state}
trajectory into disjoint reset-to-event blocks. On a block $s{:}t$, the
recurrence and $0\leq\rho_r\leq1$ give
$|\widetilde z_{ij}^{t}|\leq|z_{ij}^{s}|+\sum_{\tau=s}^{t}|a_{ij}^{\tau}|$.
At an event the left side is at least $\vartheta$. Summing the blocks, of which
only the first uses the initial state, proves~\eqref{eq:event-budget}. The bound
\rev{is pathwise: it holds for each realized sequence of local updates, without
averaging over training randomness or assuming independence. It therefore
gives a deterministic accounting link between accumulated evidence and event
traffic. The result does not imply that weak evidence must eventually fire.}

\rev{Let $\vect{g}^r=\nabla F(\vect{w}^r)$ be the global gradient at the current
server model,} and let
$N_r=\sum_i\norm{\vect{c}_i^r}_0$ denote the number of client-coordinate events in
round $r$. To distinguish pulses that agree with a descent direction from those
that oppose it, define
\begin{align}
 D_r&=\sum_{i,j:\,c_{ij}^rg_j^r<0}|g_j^r|,
 &H_r&=\sum_{i,j:\,c_{ij}^rg_j^r>0}|g_j^r| .
\end{align}
The realized aggregate alignment is then
\begin{equation}
 A_r=-\langle\vect{g}^r,\vect{C}^r\rangle=D_r-H_r,
 \label{eq:alignment}
\end{equation}
Here $D_r$ and $H_r$ are helpful and harmful global-gradient mass.
\rev{For an emitted pulse, $c_{ij}^rg_j^r<0$ means that the pulse points
opposite the gradient and hence toward first-order descent, whereas
$c_{ij}^rg_j^r>0$ means that it points with the gradient and is harmful to
first order.}
\rev{Aggregation includes same-\newrev{parameter} reinforcement and cancellation. This
is the same $A_r$ as in~\eqref{eq:alignment-decomposition}.} Coordinatewise,
\rev{the aggregate entry is $C_j^r=\sum_i c_{ij}^r$. Applying
Cauchy--Schwarz to the vectors $(1,\ldots,1)$ and
$(c_{1j}^r,\ldots,c_{Mj}^r)$ gives}
$(\sum_i c_{ij}^r)^2\leq M\sum_i(c_{ij}^r)^2$. Because
$(c_{ij}^r)^2=m_{ij}^r$, summing over $j$ yields
$\norm{\vect{C}^r}_2^2\leq MN_r$. \rev{Thus cancellation can make the actual
aggregate smaller, while $MN_r$ remains a worst-case bound determined only by
the transmitted event count.}

\newrev{$A_r$ indicates whether the aggregate update follows a first-order
descent direction, but not which mechanism causes that alignment.
To separate the effects of current local updates, \newrev{stored} encoder memory,
local optimization, and client heterogeneity, let}
$\mathcal S_i^r=\{j:c_{ij}^r\neq0\}$, \newrev{the set of parameter indices on
which client $i$ emits in round $r$}. Write $s_{ij}^r=c_{ij}^r$ on this set,
and define the local gradient
$\vect{g}_i^r=\nabla F_i(\vect{w}^r)$ and update proxy
$\vect{v}_i^r=\vect{\delta}_i^r/(\eta_rE_r)$. For emitted client-\newrev{parameter}
pairs, set
\begin{align}
 P_r&=\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal S_i^r}|v_{ij}^r|,
 &R_r&=2\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal S_i^r}|v_{ij}^r|
 \ind{s_{ij}^rv_{ij}^r<0}, \notag\\
 L_r&=\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal S_i^r}(-g_{ij}^r-v_{ij}^r)s_{ij}^r,
 &B_r&=\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal S_i^r}(g_{ij}^r-g_j^r)s_{ij}^r.
 \label{eq:alignment-components}
\end{align}
\rev{$P_r$ is the alignment obtained if every pulse follows the current
local-update proxy. The correction $R_r$ subtracts twice the proxy magnitude
when \newrev{stored} memory makes a pulse oppose that proxy. The terms $L_r$ and
$B_r$ then account, respectively, for the mismatch between the multi-step
stochastic update and the current local gradient, and between the local and
global gradients.}

\begin{proposition}[Exact alignment decomposition]
For every realized round,
\begin{equation}
 A_r=P_r-R_r+L_r+B_r.
 \label{eq:alignment-decomposition}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
For each emitted pair, add and subtract $g_{ij}^r$ and $v_{ij}^r$
from $-g_j^rs_{ij}^r$. The identity
$v_{ij}^rs_{ij}^r=|v_{ij}^r|-2|v_{ij}^r|
\ind{s_{ij}^rv_{ij}^r<0}$ then yields
\eqref{eq:alignment-decomposition} upon summation.
The mismatch terms $L_r$ and $B_r$ have unrestricted signs,
so the identity alone does not guarantee descent.

\begin{proposition}[\rev{One-round descent}]
If $F$ is $L$-smooth, the server update and the aggregate-norm bound give
\vspace{-3pt}
\begin{align}
 F(\vect{w}^{r+1})
 &\leq F(\vect{w}^r)+q_r\langle\nabla F(\vect{w}^r),\vect{C}^r\rangle
 +\frac{L}{2}q_r^2\norm{\vect{C}^r}_2^2 \notag\\
 &\leq F(\vect{w}^r)-q_rA_r+\frac{LM}{2}q_r^2N_r.
 \label{eq:descent}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}
\rev{This inequality holds pathwise. A stationarity result additionally
requires the emitted aggregate to align with global descent.}

\begin{theorem}[\reviewrev{Conditional finite-horizon gradient-norm bound}]
\label{thm:conditional-stationarity}
\rev{Fix a horizon $R$ and assume that (i) $F$ is $L$-smooth and bounded below by
$F_{\inf}$, (ii) $\vect{w}^1$ and the positive schedule
$q_1,\ldots,q_R$ are deterministic, and (iii) there are constants $\kappa>0$
and deterministic $\beta_r\geq0$ such that
}
\begin{equation}
 \rev{\E[A_r\mid\mathcal{F}_r]\geq
 \kappa\norm{\nabla F(\vect{w}^r)}_2^2-\beta_r,}
 \label{eq:alignment-assumption-paper}
\end{equation}
\rev{where $\mathcal{F}_r$ contains all randomness revealed before the local
computation in round $r$. Define $Q_R=\sum_{r=1}^Rq_r$ and independently draw
$J_R$ with $\Pr(J_R=r)=q_r/Q_R$. Then}
\begin{align}
 \E\norm{\nabla F(\vect{w}^{J_R})}_2^2
 &\leq
 \underbrace{\frac{F(\vect{w}^1)-F_{\inf}}{\kappa Q_R}}_{
   \substack{\scriptscriptstyle\text{initial}\\[-2pt]\scriptscriptstyle\text{gap}}}
 + \underbrace{\frac{\sum_{r=1}^Rq_r\beta_r}{\kappa Q_R}}_{
   \substack{\scriptscriptstyle\text{alignment}\\[-2pt]\scriptscriptstyle\text{defect}}}
 \notag\\
 &\quad+
 \underbrace{\frac{LM}{2\kappa Q_R}
 \sum_{r=1}^Rq_r^2\E N_r}_{
   \substack{\scriptscriptstyle\text{event}\\[-2pt]\scriptscriptstyle\text{curvature}}}.
 \label{eq:stationarity}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\reviewrev{The event-budget bound is pathwise, whereas
Theorem~\ref{thm:conditional-stationarity} additionally assumes conditional
expected descent alignment; this property does not follow from the encoder.
Equation~\eqref{eq:stationarity} controls a $q_r$-weighted random iterate,
not necessarily the final model. In particular, a useful small bound requires
the initial-gap term $1/Q_R$, the normalized defect
$\sum_r q_r\beta_r/Q_R$, and the normalized event-curvature term
$\sum_r q_r^2\E N_r/Q_R$ to be collectively small.
Since the theorem does not bound $\beta_r$ or establish its decay,
it does not by itself guarantee vanishing gradients.
The diagnostics below examine realized alignment and these two
event-dependent terms on finite trajectories, not the assumed
conditional expectation or an asymptotic rate.}

\rev{Taking expectations in~\eqref{eq:descent} and applying
\eqref{eq:alignment-assumption-paper}, then dividing by $\kappa q_r$, yields}
\vspace{-3pt}
\begin{align*}
 \E\norm{\nabla F(\vect{w}^r)}_2^2
 &\leq \frac{\E F(\vect{w}^r)-\E F(\vect{w}^{r+1})}
 {\kappa q_r}+\frac{\beta_r}{\kappa}\\
 &\quad+\frac{LMq_r}{2\kappa}\E N_r.
\end{align*}
\rev{Multiplying this inequality by $q_r$ and summing over $r$ telescopes the
objective values. The lower bound $F\geq F_{\inf}$ and division by $Q_R$ then
give~\eqref{eq:stationarity}. Moreover,
$\E\norm{\nabla F(\vect{w}^{J_R})}_2^2=Q_R^{-1}\sum_rq_r
\E\norm{\nabla F(\vect{w}^r)}_2^2$. Thus, $J_R$ reports a
$q_r$-weighted random iterate for analysis without changing the algorithm or
claiming that the final iterate is best.}

At selected rounds, we \rev{independently rerun training from the same seed to
the audited round} and compute full client and global gradients after the event
decision. \rev{This diagnostic recomputation} evaluates $A_r$ and
\eqref{eq:alignment-decomposition} without altering training or later events.
For audited rounds $\mathcal A$, define
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\kappa_{\mathcal A}=
 \frac{\sum_{r\in\mathcal A}q_rA_r}
 {\sum_{r\in\mathcal A}q_r\norm{\nabla F(\vect{w}^r)}_2^2}.
 \label{eq:empirical-alignment-ratio}
\end{equation}
This normalized trajectory ratio compares realized first-order descent with
gradient energy. Positivity means average $q_r$-weighted alignment. It is
neither mean $A_r$ nor theoretical
$\kappa$ (only an analogue when $\beta_r=0$). \rev{It provides a descriptive
check of alignment along the recorded finite trajectories, but it cannot
establish the conditional expectation for unseen rounds or training runs in
\eqref{eq:alignment-assumption-paper}. Section~\ref{sec:results} therefore uses
the audit to interpret observed mechanisms rather than to claim that the
assumption has been proved.}

\reviewrev{We also assess two empirical terms that map to the theorem. The
reference $\kappa_0=8$ was selected from exploratory trajectories and then held
fixed across all four audit cells. It is neither an estimate nor a lower
confidence bound for theoretical $\kappa$. We define the realized defect
$\widehat\beta_r=[\kappa_0\norm{\vect{g}^r}_2^2-A_r]_+$. Over the audited
checkpoints $\mathcal A$, we report the zero-defect frequency and
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\mathcal D}_{\mathcal A}
 =\frac{\sum_{r\in\mathcal A}q_r\widehat\beta_r}
 {\kappa_0\sum_{r\in\mathcal A}q_r},\qquad
 \widehat{\mathcal V}_{\mathcal A}
 =\frac{M\sum_{r\in\mathcal A}q_r^2N_r}
 {\sum_{r\in\mathcal A}q_r}.
 \label{eq:empirical-theorem-diagnostics}
\end{equation}
Here $\widehat{\mathcal D}_{\mathcal A}$ measures sampled violations of this
reference, while $\widehat{\mathcal V}_{\mathcal A}$ becomes the curvature term
after multiplication by $L/(2\kappa_0)$. Zero defect shows consistency at an
audited checkpoint, not the required conditional expectation for other rounds,
seeds, or tasks. Because we neither estimate global $L$ nor audit every round,
these quantities diagnose the theorem's mechanism rather than certify its
numerical bound.}

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:experiments}

\coreRev{\textbf{Purpose and common host.}
We test whether the complete protocol reduces bidirectional communication for
predictive quality against close, calibrated alternatives. The primary panels
use CIFAR-10 with a 20,570-parameter compact CNN and a 175,258-parameter
GroupNorm ResNet-14. Ten fully participating clients each hold 5,000 examples:
55\% from one dominant class and 5\% from each other class. All methods run
1,800 rounds with five local SGD steps, batch size 32, learning rate 0.05,
L2 coefficient 0.0005, and initialization seed 7777. Data, initialization,
local minibatch order and evaluation cadence are matched within each seed.
Only the communication operator and its server gain differ.}

\coreRev{\textbf{Architecture-specific calibration.}
Every method uses a development-calibrated setting for the dataset and model;
none is selected from the new common-seed test outcomes. ResNet-14 receives a
common bounded budget per method: 18 configurations screened for 600 rounds,
then the best three continued to 1,800 rounds (14,400 unique training rounds
per family). Selection minimizes validation CE on a 45,000/5,000 training split,
with traffic and configuration identifier as tie breakers. The families use
comparable accumulated budgets but different search geometries. This is
bounded development tuning, not a guarantee of global hyperparameter optimality.}

\coreRev{The compact-CNN panel preserves its earlier dataset-specific
calibrations: Event and Str\"om use validation CE; STC uses regularized training
objective; EF-TopK uses its archived training-CE selection; Dense uses the
training-objective-selected schedule. Historical budgets and selection rules
therefore differ in that panel. The ResNet panel supplies the more controlled
common-budget comparison. Full grids, continuations and selection records
accompany the frozen evidence.}

\coreRev{\textbf{Frozen operating points.}
Both Event models use $\rho=0.999$, $r_0=100$, $\alpha=0.2$ and gain one;
$(\vartheta,q_0)=(0.05,0.01)$ for the compact CNN and $(0.01,0.0025)$
for ResNet-14. Str\"om-style residual pulses use thresholds 0.05 and 0.005,
respectively, and native gain 0.10 (FedAvg-equivalent gain two). They preserve
nonleaky subtractive residuals in the common server host. Dense uses
$\gamma_r=4(1+r/100)^{-0.3}$ for the compact CNN and constant 1.5 for ResNet.
EF-TopK retains the largest 5\% and 1\% of weighted client-delta coordinates,
with gains one and four. STC uses per-tensor upload/download retention $p=1$
and gains two and four; ternarization and both error-feedback residuals remain
active, so $p=1$ is not full-precision Dense FedAvg.}

\coreRev{\textbf{Representation fairness.}
STC retains its native lossy server compressor and server residual.
EF-TopK retains float32 sparse values and client residuals, without adding a
second lossy server operator. All compressed comparators receive lossless
support encoding and exact server-update transport where applicable;
Str\"om and Event share the same pulse-codec portfolio. Thus the comparison
charges serialized representations rather than granting efficient support
coding only to the proposed method. Exact codec round trips and reconstructed
client models are checked by the runners. The accelerated CPU codec used for
the new ResNet baselines is byte-equivalent to the frozen original codec.}

\coreRev{\textbf{Confirmation and metrics.}
Each architecture has ten common partition/SGD seed pairs, distinct from its
development partition: 6000--6900/86000--86900 for the compact CNN and
7100--8000/97100--98000 for ResNet-14, in steps of 100. Confirmation fits start
fresh on all 50,000 training images and use the fixed official 10,000-image
test set. The two panels contain 100 method--seed outcomes; 40 pulse runs are
verified exact reuses. Accuracy and unregularized test CE at round 1,800 are
fixed predictive endpoints. Each run is evaluated at round one and every
60 rounds. Independent audits verify receipts, cumulative traffic prefixes,
and sampled model fingerprints without changing training.}

\coreRev{Targets 45\%, 50\%, and 55\% were declared before the corresponding
common-seed confirmation. Cost to target is the first saved per-run crossing,
without interpolation or a requirement to remain above it. All fits continue
to 1,800 rounds. These early-to-intermediate targets complement final predictive
quality; they do not measure near-optimal convergence. A missed target is
reported as censored, never as zero cost. Traffic ratios are computed within
paired seeds, summarized geometrically, and assigned descriptive unadjusted
95\% Student-$t$ intervals on the log scale. Endpoint tables show arithmetic
means and sample SDs. The shared test set has been inspected in prior studies;
new seeds are fresh runs, not a new untouched dataset.}

\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

\coreRev{\textbf{Lower communication to declared predictive accuracy.}
Event-FedAvg has lower paired geometric-mean unicast cost than every comparator
at each declared target on both architectures (Fig.~\ref{fig:calibrated-targets}).
At 50\% accuracy, every method succeeds on all ten seeds.
On the compact CNN, Event saves 57.8\% versus STC, 95.8\% versus EF-TopK,
29.2\% versus Str\"om, and 99.1\% versus Dense.
On ResNet-14, the corresponding savings are 23.4\%, 65.4\%, 51.4\%, and 98.3\%
(Table~\ref{tab:calibrated-targets}). Event uses fewer bits in every individual
50\% target pair against all four references in both panels.
The same direction holds at 45\% and 55\% in paired mean cost;
ResNet STC at 55\% has nine individual cost wins out of ten.
The benefit is therefore reproducible across the declared targets and is not
an artifact of choosing one favorable crossing after inspection.}

\coreRev{This result primarily concerns message cost rather than a universal
reduction in training rounds. Against ResNet STC at 50\%, Event crosses at the
same saved round in nine pairs and earlier in one; their mean first crossings
are rounds 306 and 312. Exact support and aggregate transport reduce the bits
needed for those updates. We therefore describe improved communication to
predictive quality rather than a general faster-convergence rate.}

\coreRev{\textbf{Final operating points and predictive tradeoffs.}
Event has the lowest total round-1,800 unicast cost in both panels and in every
paired comparison with each reference (Table~\ref{tab:calibrated-core}).
For the compact CNN it also has the largest mean accuracy, $57.55\pm0.49\%$,
at 0.499~Gbit. Relative to the closest pulse comparator, Str\"om, the final
cost saving is 3.9\% [3.0,4.8], while the accuracy difference is
$+0.27$ percentage points with interval $[-0.43,0.97]$.
Thus the final predictive difference is unresolved; the stronger distinction
is the 29.2\% lower cost to the declared 50\% target.}

\coreRev{On ResNet-14, Event reaches $75.47\pm0.57\%$ at 5.752~Gbit,
compared with $72.16\pm0.52\%$ at 8.305~Gbit for Str\"om.
The paired advantage is $+3.31$ points [2.84,3.78] with 30.7\% less traffic.
EF-TopK has the highest final mean accuracy, $76.41\pm0.87\%$,
at 12.389~Gbit. Event saves 53.6\% [53.4,53.8] but has a paired accuracy
difference of $-0.94$ points [$-1.58,-0.31$].
STC reaches $76.19\pm1.18\%$ at 6.460~Gbit; Event saves 11.0\% [10.6,11.3]
with a $-0.72$-point accuracy difference [$-1.61,0.16$].
The STC interval does not establish equivalence or noninferiority.
Test CE is reported alongside accuracy because the two metrics need not rank
methods identically. These are favorable cost--quality operating points,
not predictive dominance over every comparator.}

\coreRev{\textbf{What the communication comparison establishes.}
Savings remain after charging separate downlink copies to ten clients.
They need not hold in each direction: versus ResNet STC, Event reduces
uplink by 70.6\% but increases downlink by 47.8\% on the paired geometric scale,
yielding an 11.0\% total saving. Logical-broadcast accounting gives a larger
59.7\% total saving against this STC point, but is not the primary metric.
The result therefore does not depend on a free broadcast assumption.
Nor does it establish a globally superior rate--quality frontier:
a sparse STC development point ($p=0.05$, gain four) used less validation
traffic and had higher validation accuracy than Event, but worse validation
CE, and was not the CE-selected confirmation point. The panel compares
preselected, quality-calibrated configurations, not all possible STC budgets.}

\textbf{Mechanism audit.}
\reviewrev{Ten paired seeds isolate cross-round memory and reset
at the original 120-round operating point.}

\reviewrev{Table~\ref{tab:operator-ablation} asks whether the temporal state or
reset rule explains the selected operating point.} Removing cross-round memory
reduces paired accuracy by $11.64$ points, with a 95\% confidence interval of
$[-12.79,-10.49]$ points and a loss on every seed, although traffic is $3.3\%$
lower. Persistent accumulation therefore contributes information beyond event
rate control in this audit. Subtractive reset changes accuracy by only $+0.42$
points, with a 95\% confidence interval of $[-0.01,0.85]$ points, while using
$4.1\%$ more traffic. Thus the experiment identifies persistence as the
important mechanism at this operating point. It does not establish predictive
superiority of full reset. We retain full reset because it defines the tested
operator and yields the bounded-state invariant and disjoint blocks used in
the analysis.

\reviewrev{Table~\ref{tab:alignment-factorial} reports ten seeds
and 31 independently audited checkpoints per seed.
The displayed $\widehat\kappa_{\mathcal A}$ is the realized
trajectory ratio in~\eqref{eq:empirical-alignment-ratio},
not the theoretical $\kappa$ or a bound on it.}

\reviewrev{Across the four audited conditions,
$96.1$--$99.0\%$ of emitted aggregates have positive first-order
alignment, but the fraction of objective decreases falls to
$69.7\%$ under strong non-IID data with five local steps.
Thus favorable sampled alignment is not sufficient for actual
descent. At the exploratory reference $\kappa_0=8$, the
reported $\widehat{\mathcal D}_{\mathcal A}$ and
$\widehat{\mathcal V}_{\mathcal A}$ diagnose, respectively,
sampled alignment shortfall and event curvature.
A post-hoc sensitivity check over $\kappa_0\in\{4,8,12\}$
gives $75.2$--$95.5\%$ zero-defect checkpoints and
$\widehat{\mathcal D}_{\mathcal A}=0.020$--$0.073$.
Neither these finite sampled ratios nor positive realized
alignment validate the conditional expectation
in~\eqref{eq:alignment-assumption-paper} or yield a numerical
certificate without a global smoothness bound.}

\reviewrev{A retrospective theory-to-trajectory audit makes this
distinction quantitative: positive alignment without an objective
decrease occurs at $29.4\%$ of the sampled strong-non-IID,
five-local-step checkpoints, versus $0.0\%$ for IID data with one
local step. Across the four conditions, mean realized curvature
remainders increase with the sampled event-scale diagnostic.
These associations motivate retaining both terms in
\eqref{eq:stationarity}, but do not certify its conditional
expectation or a global smoothness bound. The per-seed contrasts
and diagnostic plot are provided in the supplementary artifact.}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\coreRev{The primary evidence comprises two CIFAR-10 architectures, ten
synchronous clients, and one development partition per architecture. Each
method is tuned for its dataset/model, with a common bounded development budget
on ResNet; historical compact-CNN selection rules differ. Ten paired seeds
measure run variation, not hyperparameter-selection uncertainty or simultaneous
inference. STC and EF-TopK select the largest screened ResNet gain, four, so the
bounded search need not contain their optima. The selected configurations do
not characterize complete communication--quality frontiers. Broader datasets and model families remain
to be evaluated under this common calibration protocol. The targets are sampled finite-training
crossings and can be transient. The theorem assumes aggregate alignment and
does not establish an unconditional convergence rate. Supporting mechanism and
partial-participation studies use earlier frozen settings. All communication
costs describe serialized payload accounting; network latency, loss, transport
headers and hardware energy are not measured.}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\coreRev{Event-FedAvg combines persistent leaky evidence, full reset, signed
coordinate events, independent server quanta, and exact aggregate transport.
The completed calibrated CIFAR panels identify its clearest advantage: lower
total unicast communication to each declared predictive-accuracy target and
the lowest final communication among the five selected operating points.
It also gives the largest mean final compact-CNN accuracy and a clear ResNet
improvement over residual pulses. ResNet STC and EF-TopK retain higher mean
final accuracy at greater communication cost, defining the scope of the
advantage. State and event-budget bounds characterize the encoder; optimization
bounds remain conditional on aggregate alignment.}
