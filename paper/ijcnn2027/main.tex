\begin{abstract}
Federated learning repeatedly exchanges high-dimensional updates, although
individual coordinates may carry weak but temporally persistent evidence. We
introduce Event-FedAvg, a stateful communication layer inspired by leaky
integrate-and-fire dynamics. Clients accumulate weighted local-model deltas,
emit signed coordinate events after first passage, and fully reset fired
states. The server applies an independently scheduled model quantum and
synchronizes clients through exact sparse replay or dense checkpoints. We prove
a pathwise event budget and bounded encoder state, and derive a one-step descent
decomposition with a conditional finite-horizon stationarity result. An exact
pulse-level decomposition and a controlled $2\times2$ audit expose how data
heterogeneity and local-update depth affect finite-trajectory alignment. Under
matched local learning, development-only tuning, and conservative
uplink-plus-downlink accounting, Event-FedAvg is nondominated on held-out
Fashion-MNIST MLP/CNN and CIFAR-10 compact-CNN experiments. On CIFAR-10 it
reaches $48.06\pm1.12\%$ accuracy at $201.5\pm4.3$ Mbit, compared with
$44.13\pm1.12\%$ at $1586.6$ Mbit for development-tuned dense FedAvg. These
results establish a
communication--performance operating point in the evaluated synchronous
setting, not unconditional convergence or hardware-energy savings.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Federated learning (FL) allows a fleet of clients to improve a shared model
without collecting their raw data at one location. Keeping data local,
however, does not make the optimization process communication-free. Every
training round may require high-dimensional client uploads followed by a server
message that brings all clients to the next common model. For bandwidth-limited
edge systems, this repeated exchange can become the main scaling bottleneck of
federated averaging (FedAvg) \cite{mcmahan2017fedavg}.

The difficulty is not only that one update vector is large. A coordinate can be
too weak to justify transmission in a single round while remaining consistent
over several rounds. Round-wise compression does not directly exploit this
temporal persistence. Error-feedback methods preserve discarded residuals for
later correction, but their purpose is to conserve compression error. This
leads to a different design question. Can each client integrate weak coordinate
evidence over time and communicate only when that evidence crosses a threshold?

We answer this question with Event-FedAvg, a stateful communication layer for a
conventional model and local optimizer. Each client accumulates weighted local
model changes in leaky states. A threshold crossing produces a signed
coordinate event and clears the corresponding state. A separate model quantum
determines the size of the server update. The separation between the trigger
threshold and update magnitude is important because the amount of evidence
needed to communicate need not equal the step that should be applied to the
global model.

Sparse client uploads alone are not a complete FL protocol. The resulting
server update must also reach every client before the next local computation.
Event-FedAvg therefore combines event uploads with exact ordered replay and a
dense-checkpoint fallback. This closes the downlink and keeps all clients on the
same model trajectory.

Our contributions are threefold. First, we define the complete client encoder,
server update, and synchronization algorithm for multi-step FedAvg. Second, we
prove unconditional state and event-budget guarantees. We also give an exact
one-step descent inequality and a conditional finite-horizon stationarity
result, then connect its alignment condition to measurable local-update,
memory, drift, and heterogeneity terms. Third, we compare Event-FedAvg with
dense FedAvg, Sign-EF, EF-TopK, and
Strom-style threshold pulses under matched local learning, held-out non-IID
partitions, and total bidirectional traffic. Event-FedAvg is nondominated in all
three tested model and dataset settings. On CIFAR-10, it uses $7.9\times$ less
traffic than dense FedAvg while improving mean accuracy by $3.93$ percentage
points. These claims are restricted to three-seed, synchronous
full-participation experiments and software-level bit accounting.

\section{Related Work and Novelty Boundary}
\label{sec:related-work}

One-bit and sign methods reduce precision, while sparse methods with memory and
error feedback preserve compression residuals for later correction
\cite{seide2014onebit,bernstein2018signsgd,stich2018memory,richtarik2021ef21}.
STC applies residual-corrected sparse ternary compression in both FL directions
\cite{sattler2020stc}. Strom's coordinate residuals and signed threshold pulses
are our closest algorithmic predecessor \cite{strom2015distributed}. Unlike its
conservative residual subtraction, Event-FedAvg leaks old evidence, fully
clears fired coordinates, and decouples the trigger threshold from the applied
model quantum.

Event-triggered learning usually suppresses a vector message until a model or
gradient deviation becomes significant. Examples include self-triggered memory
uploads, compressed decentralized triggers, and bidirectional event-triggered
FL \cite{ghadikolaei2021lena,singh2023sparq,he2023etfl,yoon2025etfl,
zhai2026gossip}. In our dated, targeted review, we did not identify a method
combining coordinate first passage, leaky evidence, full reset, and an
independent server quantum. This is an absence-search result, not proof of
priority. Federated SNN work,
in turn, places LIF dynamics inside the trained model and communicates SNN
parameters or compressed updates \cite{venkatesha2021fedsnn,chaki2023tradeoffs,
tao2026sfedhifi}. We instead use LIF-inspired state only as a communication
layer around conventional FedAvg. Accordingly, we claim the complete operator
and protocol, not priority for event-triggered, sign, or neuromorphic FL.

\section{Event-FedAvg}
\label{sec:method}

This section introduces the quantities in the order in which they appear in one
communication round. We first define local training, then the client event
encoder, and finally the server update and downlink synchronization.

\subsection{Round notation and local update}

Let rounds be indexed by $r\in\{1,\ldots,R\}$, clients by
$i\in\{1,\ldots,M\}$, and model coordinates by $j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}$.
The server model at the start of round $r$ is $w^r\in\mathbb{R}^d$, and every
client starts its local computation from this same model. Client $i$ has local
objective $F_i$ and aggregation weight $p_i>0$, with $\sum_i p_i=1$. The global
objective is
\begin{equation}
 F(w)=\sum_{i=1}^M p_iF_i(w).
\end{equation}
After setting $w_{i,0}^r=w^r$, client $i$ performs $E_r$ local SGD steps with
learning rate $\eta_r>0$:
\begin{align}
 w_{i,e+1}^r&=w_{i,e}^r-\eta_r g_{i,e}^r,
 \qquad e=0,\ldots,E_r-1, \notag\\
 \delta_i^r&=w_{i,E_r}^r-w^r
 =-\eta_r\sum_{e=0}^{E_r-1}g_{i,e}^r .
 \label{eq:local-delta}
\end{align}
Here $g_{i,e}^r$ is the stochastic gradient used by client $i$ at local iterate
$w_{i,e}^r$. The vector $\delta_i^r$ is therefore the complete local model
change supplied to the communication layer.

\subsection{Client event encoder}

Figure~\ref{fig:event-fedavg-method} summarizes the round transition and its two
persistent quantities, namely the post-reset encoder state and the exactly
synchronized server model.

Client $i$ carries a post-reset encoder state
$z_i^r\in\mathbb{R}^d$ into round $r$. All experiments initialize
$z_i^1=0$. The weighted evidence input $a_i^r$ and the state immediately before
the threshold test, $\widetilde z_i^r$, are
\begin{equation}
 a_i^r=\frac{p_i}{\eta_r}\delta_i^r,
 \qquad
 \widetilde z_i^r=\rho_r z_i^r+a_i^r,
 \quad 0\leq\rho_r\leq1 .
 \label{eq:evidence-update}
\end{equation}
The factor $p_i$ inserts the FedAvg aggregation weight before thresholding, and
division by $\eta_r$ expresses the local change on a gradient scale. Client
weights therefore affect firing through evidence accumulation and are not
applied a second time at the server. The leak factor $\rho_r$ controls how much
of the previous state survives. For a fixed trigger threshold
$\vartheta>0$, every coordinate is tested exactly once:
\begin{align}
 m_{ij}^r&=\ind{|\widetilde z_{ij}^r|\geq\vartheta},
 &c_{ij}^r&=m_{ij}^r\operatorname{sign}(\widetilde z_{ij}^r),
 \label{eq:event-pulse}\\
 z_i^{r+1}&=(\mathbf{1}-m_i^r)\odot\widetilde z_i^r,
 &c_i^r&\in\{-1,0,1\}^d .
 \label{eq:full-reset}
\end{align}
The binary mask $m_i^r$ records which coordinates fire, while $c_i^r$ contains
the signed pulses sent by the client. Equation~\eqref{eq:full-reset} is a full
reset. A fired coordinate discards its entire pre-trigger state, including
overshoot. It neither subtracts the
threshold nor retains a compression residual. This is the principal semantic
difference from residual-conserving threshold pulses.

\subsection{Server update and synchronization}

The server sums the client pulses into $C^r\in\mathbb{Z}^d$. With a positive
model quantum $q_r$, common to all coordinates in a round but independent of
$\vartheta$, it applies
\begin{equation}
 C^r=\sum_{i=1}^M c_i^r,
 \qquad
 w^{r+1}=w^r+q_rC^r .
 \label{eq:server-update}
\end{equation}
The experiments prescribe $q_r=q_0(1+r/r_0)^{-\alpha}$, where $q_0>0$ is the
initial quantum, $r_0>0$ is a round scale, and $\alpha\geq0$ is the decay
exponent. Clients recover $q_r$
from the round index and transmit no floating-point event magnitude.
Coincident client pulses add algebraically and may cancel. There is no
normalization by messages or events.

A nonempty client packet carries one 64-bit header and, per event, a
$\lceil\log_2d\rceil$-bit address and one sign bit. To close the round, the
server returns either the ordered update stream or a dense float32 checkpoint,
whichever is cheaper. The clients reproduce $w^{r+1}$ exactly. The reported
conservative total additionally
charges initial synchronization and a 32-bit catch-up request plus the chosen
downlink representation for every client and round. Replay and checkpointing
change communication cost, not the mathematical update in
\eqref{eq:server-update}. Algorithm~\ref{alg:event-fedavg} states the complete
ordering used in all final experiments.

\begin{algorithm}[Event-FedAvg communication layer]
\label{alg:event-fedavg}
\normalfont\small
Initialize $w^1$ and $z_i^1=0$. Fix $p_i$, $\vartheta$, and the schedules
$\eta_r$, $\rho_r$, and $q_r$. For each round $r=1,\ldots,R$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Every client starts from $w^r$, performs $E_r$ local SGD steps, and
  forms $\delta_i^r$ using~\eqref{eq:local-delta}.
  \item Client $i$ computes
  $\widetilde z_i^r=\rho_rz_i^r+(p_i/\eta_r)\delta_i^r$ and forms the mask
  $m_i^r$ and signed pulse vector $c_i^r$ using~\eqref{eq:event-pulse}.
  \item Client $i$ resets the fired states using~\eqref{eq:full-reset} and
  sends the indices and signs of the nonzero entries of $c_i^r$.
  \item The server forms $C^r=\sum_i c_i^r$ and updates
  $w^{r+1}=w^r+q_rC^r$.
  \item The server returns an exact ordered replay or, when cheaper, a dense
  checkpoint so that every client starts the next round from $w^{r+1}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Scoped Theoretical Characterization}
\label{sec:theory}

We separate encoder properties that hold for every realized input sequence
from the assumption needed to connect emitted events to optimization.

\begin{proposition}[State and pathwise event budget]
Assume $|z_{ij}^1|<\vartheta$ initially. The full-reset encoder keeps
$|z_{ij}^r|<\vartheta$ in every round. If
$N_R^{\rm coord}=\sum_{r=1}^R\sum_{i,j}m_{ij}^r$, then
\begin{equation}
 N_R^{\rm coord}\leq\frac{1}{\vartheta}
 \left(\sum_i\norm{z_i^1}_1+
 \sum_{r=1}^R\sum_i\norm{a_i^r}_1\right).
 \label{eq:event-budget}
\end{equation}
Consequently, if $P_R$ is the number of nonempty packets, the exact event
uplink is $64P_R+(\lceil\log_2d\rceil+1)N_R^{\rm coord}$ bits.
\end{proposition}
The state claim follows immediately from the threshold test and full reset.
For~\eqref{eq:event-budget}, expand the leaky recurrence over each disjoint
reset-to-event block. Every leak product is at most one, while threshold
crossing requires at least $\vartheta$ total absolute evidence in that block.
This bound is deterministic and permits biased, dependent, or nonstationary
inputs. It is an upper bound, not a guarantee that weak evidence fires.

Write $g^r=\nabla F(w^r)$ and let
$N_r=\sum_i\norm{c_i^r}_0$ denote the number of client-coordinate events in
round $r$. To distinguish pulses that agree with a descent direction from those
that oppose it, define
\begin{align}
 D_r&=\sum_{i,j:\,c_{ij}^rg_j^r<0}|g_j^r|,
 &H_r&=\sum_{i,j:\,c_{ij}^rg_j^r>0}|g_j^r| .
\end{align}
The realized aggregate alignment is then
\begin{equation}
 A_r=-\langle g^r,C^r\rangle=D_r-H_r,
 \label{eq:alignment}
\end{equation}
where $D_r$ and $H_r$ are respectively the global-gradient mass carried by
client pulses opposing and following $g^r$. This identity includes
same-coordinate cancellation. Cauchy--Schwarz also gives
$\norm{C^r}_2^2\leq MN_r$.

To expose the mechanisms hidden inside $A_r$, let
$\mathcal S_i^r=\{j:c_{ij}^r\neq0\}$, write $s_{ij}^r=c_{ij}^r$ on this set,
and define the current local gradient $g_i^r=\nabla F_i(w^r)$ and local-update
proxy $v_i^r=\delta_i^r/(\eta_rE_r)$. For emitted client-coordinate pairs, set
\begin{align}
 P_r&=\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal S_i^r}|v_{ij}^r|,
 &R_r&=2\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal S_i^r}|v_{ij}^r|
 \ind{s_{ij}^rv_{ij}^r<0}, \notag\\
 L_r&=\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal S_i^r}(-g_{ij}^r-v_{ij}^r)s_{ij}^r,
 &B_r&=\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal S_i^r}(g_{ij}^r-g_j^r)s_{ij}^r.
 \label{eq:alignment-components}
\end{align}
$P_r$ is the alignment with the current local-update proxy, $R_r$ is the
penalty from accumulated state firing against that proxy, $L_r$ contains local
drift and stochastic-gradient mismatch, and $B_r$ contains client-to-global
heterogeneity.

\begin{proposition}[Exact alignment decomposition]
For every realized round,
\begin{equation}
 A_r=P_r-R_r+L_r+B_r.
 \label{eq:alignment-decomposition}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
The identity follows by adding and subtracting $v_{ij}^r$ and $g_{ij}^r$ for
each emitted pair. The signs of $L_r$ and $B_r$ are unrestricted, so the
decomposition identifies the alignment sources but does not by itself prove a
positive lower bound.

\begin{proposition}[Descent and conditional stationarity]
If $F$ is $L$-smooth, every realized round obeys
\begin{equation}
 F(w^{r+1})\leq F(w^r)-q_rA_r+\frac{LM}{2}q_r^2N_r.
 \label{eq:descent}
\end{equation}
Let $\mathcal{F}_r$ contain all randomness revealed before round-$r$ local
computation, and suppose, for $\kappa>0$ and deterministic $\beta_r\geq0$,
\begin{equation}
 \E[A_r\mid\mathcal{F}_r]\geq
 \kappa\norm{\nabla F(w^r)}_2^2-\beta_r .
 \label{eq:alignment-assumption-paper}
\end{equation}
If $F\geq F_{\inf}$ and $w^1$ is deterministic, define
$Q_R=\sum_{r=1}^Rq_r$ and independently draw $J_R$ with
$\Pr(J_R=r)=q_r/Q_R$. Then
\begin{align}
 \kappa Q_R\E\norm{\nabla F(w^{J_R})}_2^2
 &\leq F(w^1)-F_{\inf}+\sum_rq_r\beta_r \notag\\
 &\quad+\frac{LM}{2}\sum_rq_r^2\E N_r .
 \label{eq:stationarity}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}
Equation~\eqref{eq:descent} is the smoothness inequality combined with
\eqref{eq:alignment} and the event-energy bound. Summing its conditional
expectation proves~\eqref{eq:stationarity}. The result is deliberately
conditional. Silence, leakage, stale evidence, local drift, sign errors, and
heterogeneity all enter $\beta_r$. The empirical power exponents $0.1$ and
$0.3$ are finite-horizon schedules and do not satisfy the infinite-horizon
square-summability condition, so we make no asymptotic convergence claim for
the reported runs.

We audit~\eqref{eq:alignment-assumption-paper} without changing the training
rule. For audited rounds $\mathcal A$, define the finite-trajectory statistic
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\kappa_{\mathcal A}=
 \frac{\sum_{r\in\mathcal A}q_rA_r}
 {\sum_{r\in\mathcal A}q_r\norm{\nabla F(w^r)}_2^2}.
 \label{eq:empirical-alignment-ratio}
\end{equation}
Section~\ref{sec:results} evaluates this statistic and
\eqref{eq:alignment-decomposition} across data heterogeneity and local-update
depth. These finite-trajectory diagnostics assess the conditional interface.
They do not prove the conditional expectation in
\eqref{eq:alignment-assumption-paper}.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:experiments}

\textbf{Tasks and models.}
We evaluate ten-client image classification under strong label skew. The
Fashion-MNIST campaign uses a 25,818-parameter MLP for 150 rounds and a
14,538-parameter compact CNN for 80 rounds. CIFAR-10 uses a conventional
20,570-parameter CNN for 120 rounds. It has two strided convolutional layers with
8 and 16 channels, a 32-unit hidden layer, and a ten-class output. No model is
spiking. Each CIFAR-10 client holds 5,000 examples: 2,750 from its dominant
class and 250 from every other class. All runs use ten active clients, five
local SGD steps, minibatches of 32, and identical initialization and minibatch
streams across methods for a given seed. The local learning rate is $0.1$ on
Fashion-MNIST and $0.05$ on CIFAR-10.

\textbf{Selection and evaluation.}
We compare Event-FedAvg with dense FedAvg, one-bit Sign-EF, EF-TopK with error
feedback, and Strom's residual-conserving threshold pulses. Hyperparameters are
selected only on one development partition per dataset. For each baseline we
retain its best-quality point. This includes a CIFAR-10 dense-server gain
selected from $\{0.25,0.5,0.75,1,1.5,2,4\}$ by development training loss.
For Strom and EF-TopK we additionally freeze the development point closest to
Event-FedAvg in log total traffic. Final values are mean and sample standard
deviation over three independently seeded data realizations of the same
predeclared strong-skew template, with paired training seeds across methods.
Test cross-entropy (CE), accuracy, worst-class accuracy, coordinate events, and
communication are recorded. No held-out result influences selection.

\textbf{Communication metric.}
The primary metric is conservative bidirectional unicast traffic. It charges
packet headers, coordinate addresses, signs or values, initial dense model
synchronization, a 32-bit request per client and round, and either exact ordered
update replay or a float32 checkpoint, whichever is smaller. The same rule is
applied to every method. Thus a sparse uplink is not credited while omitting the
communication required to keep all client models synchronized.

\textbf{Alignment factorial.}
On the Fashion-MNIST MLP, we additionally cross IID and strong non-IID
partitions with $E\in\{1,5\}$ while keeping all Event-FedAvg settings fixed.
For each of the same three seed pairs, rounds $1,5,10,\ldots,150$ are
independently replayed. Exact gradients use all 6,000 examples of every client
and are evaluated only after the audited event decision, preventing the
diagnostic computation from perturbing later threshold crossings.

\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

Figure~\ref{fig:communication-frontier} gives the primary cross-benchmark
comparison using total uplink-plus-downlink unicast traffic. Filled controls
are quality-selected on development partitions. Hollow controls are selected
for proximity to Event-FedAvg traffic. Table~\ref{tab:main-results} isolates
the strongest-quality and nearest-traffic controls under those predeclared
rules.

On Fashion-MNIST MLP, Event-FedAvg attains
$83.14\pm0.14\%$ at $183.5\pm2.0$ Mbit. The strongest quality-selected
control, EF-TopK, reaches $82.45\pm0.19\%$ using $1010.5$ Mbit, whereas the
nearest-traffic Strom point reaches $79.51\pm0.86\%$ at
$181.0\pm6.7$ Mbit. The CNN exposes the important qualification. Strom gains
$0.90$ accuracy points, but requires $359.5\pm4.3$ Mbit versus
$72.8\pm1.2$ Mbit. At nearby traffic, Event-FedAvg exceeds Strom by
$1.46$ points.

The independent CIFAR-10 campaign supports transfer of the operating point.
Event-FedAvg obtains the best mean accuracy and cross-entropy among the
quality-selected methods while using the least traffic. Compared with dense
FedAvg tuned to server gain $2$, its $48.06\pm1.12\%$ accuracy is
$3.93$ points higher at only $12.7\%$ of the traffic. Tuned dense FedAvg
reaches $44.13\pm1.12\%$. Quality-selected Strom reaches
$47.68\pm0.49\%$ but uses
$4.6\times$ more traffic. Its nearest-traffic variant uses $10.1\%$ more
traffic and trails by $12.56$ points. The nearest EF-TopK point is a genuine
trade-off. It uses $32.9\%$ less traffic but trails by $7.10$ points.

The targeted mechanism audit narrows the LIF interpretation. A like-for-like
rerun of the frozen Event-FedAvg setting gives $48.25\pm1.07\%$ at
$200.2\pm2.2$ Mbit. Removing leakage ($\rho=1$) gives
$48.32\pm1.05\%$ at $202.1\pm5.6$ Mbit. Thus leakage at $\rho=0.999$ has no
material isolated effect at this resolution. In contrast, coupling the server
quantum to the trigger as $q_r=\vartheta=0.025$ reduces accuracy to
$18.42\pm7.33\%$ at $44.8\pm17.7$ Mbit. This supports independent trigger and
update resolutions for the selected operating point, not a universal claim
that every coupled schedule fails.

Table~\ref{tab:alignment-factorial} connects the optimization interface to its
mechanisms. Mean aggregate alignment is positive in all four cells, and
$95.7$--$100.0\%$ of the audited updates have $A_r>0$. Heterogeneity has the
predicted adverse effect on $B_r$ for every paired seed. After normalizing
$q_r$-weighted signed sums by
$\sum_{r\in\mathcal A}q_r\norm{g^r}_2^2$, its mean changes from $-0.27$ to
$-360.98$ at $E=1$ and from $-5.37$ to $-700.62$ at $E=5$. Local depth also
changes $L_r$, but the direction depends on heterogeneity: its normalized mean
moves from $-21.74$ to $-57.80$ under IID data and from $-95.23$ to $411.36$
under strong non-IID data. Thus local drift and heterogeneity interact rather
than contributing independent worst-case penalties.

Positive first-order alignment is not sufficient for monotone descent. Under
strong non-IID data, the objective-decrease fraction falls from $86.0\%$ at
$E=1$ to $67.7\%$ at $E=5$, even though aggregate alignment remains positive
in $98.9\%$ of the latter snapshots. The mean curvature remainder increases
from $0.0100$ to $0.0166$, and $29$ of the $93$ strong-non-IID, $E=5$ updates
have positive alignment without objective descent. No emitted pulse opposes
the current local-update proxy in any of the $372$ audited snapshots. The
experiment therefore supports the decomposition and identifies a finite-step
heterogeneity--depth limitation. It does not close the conditional convergence
assumption.

The frontier is not uniform predictive dominance. On CIFAR-10,
quality-selected EF-TopK obtains slightly higher mean worst-class accuracy
($26.7\%$) than Event-FedAvg ($24.6\%$). Tuned dense FedAvg reaches $23.3\%$.
All estimates use
only three held-out seeds. Together with the alignment audit in
Section~\ref{sec:theory}, the results support the proposed finite-horizon
communication mechanism, but do not establish the conditional assumption or
statistical significance.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The experiments cover synchronous full participation, ten clients, two image
datasets, and three held-out seeds per setting. The factorial audit varies two
factors around one Fashion-MNIST operating point and is not a broad robustness
study. The results do not establish
statistical significance or broad dataset and model generalization. The theory
does not prove the aggregate-alignment condition for the coupled learning
process, and the evaluated quantum schedules do not support an asymptotic
convergence claim. Communication is counted in software-level bits under an
exact synchronization protocol. We do not measure latency, packet loss,
asynchronous execution, or neuromorphic-hardware energy.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Event-FedAvg turns temporally accumulated local-update evidence into sparse
signed events using optional leakage, full reset, and an independently chosen
model quantum. Its encoder admits pathwise state and event-budget guarantees,
while the optimization interface is exposed through an explicit alignment
condition. Across the tested Fashion-MNIST and CIFAR-10 settings, the method
provides a strong bidirectional communication--performance operating point.
The results motivate broader evaluation under partial participation,
asynchrony, and measured communication systems.
