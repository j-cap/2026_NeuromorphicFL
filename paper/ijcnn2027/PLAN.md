# IJCNN 2027 submission plan

This document is the working plan for extracting an IJCNN conference paper
from the NeuromorphicFL living report. It is deliberately gate-based rather
than date-based: a stage closes when its evidence and deliverables are ready.

The detailed `report/` remains the project knowledge base. The paper should be
a concise publication artifact and must not inherit the report's chronological
experiment-log structure.

## Paper contract

### Central question

Can a leaky integrate-and-fire-inspired, stateful communication layer provide
a useful communication--performance operating point for conventional
multi-step federated averaging?

### Intended central claim

Event-FedAvg accumulates weighted local-update evidence in persistent leaky
states, emits sparse signed coordinate events after first passage, fully resets
fired coordinates, and applies an independently scheduled model quantum. Under
matched local learning and conservative bidirectional accounting, this operator
occupies a strong communication--performance operating point relative to close
residual-pulse and conventional compression baselines.

### Supporting claims

1. **Algorithmic:** the complete operator is distinct from residual-conserving
   threshold-pulse compression through leakage, full reset, and the separation
   of trigger threshold from model-update resolution.
2. **Theoretical:** the encoder has bounded state and pathwise event complexity;
   smoothness yields a one-step descent decomposition and a conditional
   finite-horizon stationarity result.
3. **Empirical:** Event-FedAvg remains competitive under matched training,
   held-out partitions, close tuned baselines, and total uplink-plus-downlink
   communication accounting.

### Claims explicitly excluded

- unconditional convergence of the complete method;
- uniform predictive superiority over every compressor operating point;
- measured neuromorphic-hardware energy savings;
- novelty of accumulation, thresholding, sign transmission, or event-triggered
  learning in isolation;
- broad generality based on Fashion-MNIST alone.

**Gate:** classify the result before writing the claim:

- **pass:** Event-FedAvg remains on the communication--performance frontier;
- **qualified pass:** it trades some quality for a clear communication gain;
- **fail:** it is dominated at comparable traffic, requiring an explicitly
  narrower paper claim.

**Decision:** **pass**. Event-FedAvg obtains 48.06$\pm$1.12\% mean test
accuracy at 201.5$\pm$4.3 Mbit. It is nondominated, uses about 7.9 times less
traffic than dense FedAvg while improving mean accuracy by 5.63 percentage
points, and remains favorable to the closest quality-selected and
traffic-neighbor controls. This is a three-seed operating-point result, not a
statistical-significance or uniform worst-class-superiority claim.

## Scope-control rules

1. The conference paper is an extraction project, not a continuation of the
   complete research log.
2. The second benchmark is the default final scientific experiment.
3. T5 is conditional on a concrete reviewer-facing theory need.
4. Larger models, partial participation, real network experiments, hardware
   energy measurements, and stronger coupled convergence theory belong to a
   possible journal extension.
5. New work enters the conference scope only when it closes a documented
   correctness, novelty, or evidence gap.

## Definition of submission-ready

The work is ready for IJCNN submission when:

- [ ] the complete paper fits the official format and page limit;
- [ ] every contribution has direct evidence;
- [ ] every reported value is reproducible and traceable;
- [ ] the closest predecessors and limitations are represented accurately;
- [ ] the communication metric is symmetric and consistently defined;
- [ ] the theory states its assumptions and boundaries explicitly;
- [x] the second benchmark supports at least a defensible qualified-pass claim;
- [ ] all figures and tables are referenced, readable, and necessary;
- [ ] independent technical reviews have no unresolved blocking comments;
- [ ] the final code, results, and manuscript are frozen together.
